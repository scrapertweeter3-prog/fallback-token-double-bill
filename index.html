A dead attempt still bills at the primary rate

Teams add a cheap fallback model to catch timeouts: when the primary stalls, the same context goes to a smaller model and the session keeps moving. Price the pair at a normal spread, $3 per million input tokens on the primary and $0.60 on the fallback. A working session carries an 18,000-token context, the stack makes 2,500 calls a day, and the fallback fires on 12% of them.

On paper the blend saves money. Every call on the primary costs 18,000 tokens at $3/M, or $0.054, so an all-primary day is $135. Split the day 2,200 primary and 300 fallback and the bill is $118.80 plus $3.24, $122.04, about 9.6% under the baseline. That is the number that goes in the migration doc.

The paper misses two charges. Fallbacks fire on the slow cases, the long contexts, and those average 31,000 tokens, so the cheap side of the ledger is $5.58 at $0.60/M. The primary also read its full context before it stalled, and input tokens are billed on a call that returns nothing: 300 dead attempts at $0.054 is $16.20. The real day is $140.58, which is 4.1% more than running the whole stack on the primary and paying for zero fallbacks.

The swap itself is still right. Resending a 31,000-token context to the primary costs $0.093; the same context on the fallback costs $0.019, so cheap beats retry. The leak is sequencing: cut over at the first stalled chunk instead of after the read completes, and the dead attempt bills 6,000 tokens instead of 18,000, $5.40 across the day's failures. The blended day lands at $129.78 and the fallback earns its 3.9%.
